Research Presentation Details

Multi-Horizon Forecasting of Cardiovascular Medication Utilisation During Flood Disasters: A Comparison of XGBoost, LSTM and Temporal Fusion Transformer

Ts. Mohd Radzi bin Ab. Aziz1,2, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Hanis Hanum Zulkifly1, Dr. Abdul Haniff Mohamad Yahaya2, Assoc. Prof. Dr. Fazlin Mohd Fauzi1

1 Faculty of Pharmacy, Universiti Teknologi MARA (UiTM), 42300 Puncak Alam, Selangor, Malaysia
2 Pharmacy Services Programme (PPF), Ministry of Health Malaysia (KKM), 46350 Petaling Jaya, Selangor, Malaysia

Research Abstract

Background

Flood disasters disrupt healthcare delivery and pharmaceutical supply chains, threatening continuity of care for chronic conditions such as hypertension and hypercholesterolaemia. Accurate forecasting of medication utilisation across different forecast horizons is therefore important to support disaster preparedness and medication resource planning in public healthcare facilities.

Objectives

This study aimed to compare the forecasting performance of XGBoost, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) models for predicting cardiovascular medication utilisation across 7-, 14-, 21-, and 28-day horizons during flood disasters.

Methods

Daily dispensing data for amlodipine, perindopril, and simvastatin from 24 public healthcare facilities in flood-prone districts of Malaysia (2017–2022) were integrated with hydrological, meteorological, and flood-related variables. The three forecasting models were developed and evaluated using an out-of-sample 2023 test set. Performance accuracy was assessed using Symmetric Mean Absolute Percentage Error (sMAPE) and Mean Absolute Scaled Error (MASE) across overall, flood, and non-flood periods.

Results

All models outperformed the naïve forecasting baseline (MASE < 1), indicating that cardiovascular medication utilisation could be reliably forecast across all horizons. Among the three models, LSTM and TFT consistently achieved lower forecasting errors than XGBoost, with predictive accuracy improving as the forecast horizon increased. TFT achieved the best overall performance across the 14- to 28-day horizons, with the lowest error at the 28-day horizon (sMAPE 2.09%; MASE 0.1265). Similar performance rankings were observed during flood periods, with TFT maintaining the highest forecasting accuracy under disaster conditions.

Conclusion

Deep learning models, particularly TFT, demonstrated superior and more consistent forecasting performance than XGBoost across multiple horizons. These findings support the application of TFT for medium- to long-term medication planning in Malaysian public healthcare facilities.
